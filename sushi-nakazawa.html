DMVFood / Write-Ups / Sushi Nakazawa
Penn Quarter, DC

Sushi Nakazawa brings its omakase counter to the district — and it earns the seat.

A tuna-forward tasting that builds course by course, from lean akami to fatty otoro, with a seared piece and a citrus-bright white fish to break the richness.

Rating
5.0
Cuisine
Japanese Omakase
Price
$$$$
Neighborhood
Penn Quarter, DC

Sushi Nakazawa's DC counter runs a nigiri progression the way it should be run: quietly, in order, letting the fish do the talking. No pageantry, no narration for narration's sake — just a chef working through a sequence built to show range, one cut at a time.

The tuna arc

The counter opens on lean akami — clean, almost mineral, the kind of cut that tells you immediately whether the rest of the omakase is going to be worth the seat. It builds from there: chutoro next, softer and marbled, then into otoro, the fattiest cut, practically dissolving before you can chew it. Watching the color shift plate to plate, from deep red to a pale, marbled pink, is half the point.

The tuna alone would be worth the counter seat — everything after it is a bonus.

Beyond the tuna

A lightly seared piece breaks up the run of raw cuts — a quick torch over the surface, just enough to render a thin layer of fat without cooking through, laid over a smear of something sharp underneath. Further down the board, a delicate white fish arrives dusted with citrus zest, a clean counterpoint after the richness of the tuna courses.

The Verdict

A splurge-worthy counter that earns its price point on fish quality alone. Book ahead — the seats go fast.
